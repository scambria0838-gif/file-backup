# CLOTHING & APPEARANCE TESTIMONY — FULL REPORT
## State v. Lenroy Tony Johnson — Case No. CR94-03264A
## Maricopa County Superior Court — Hon. Ruth H. Hilliard
### Compiled from OCR Trial Transcript (July 18–25, 1995) + Vernon Johnson Interview

---

## OVERVIEW

The clothing and physical description of the suspect seen at the fire scene (Park Lee Apartments, 4737 N. 16th Avenue, April 6, 1994) is a critical area of dispute. Multiple witnesses gave conflicting descriptions — in race, clothing, age, and build — and those descriptions evolved and changed between the night of the fire and trial testimony over a year later. This report documents every statement on record.

---

## 1. SELINA SALAZAR — Neighbor Witness (Saw person at the fire)

**Direct Examination — Trial:**

> *"Just that he had a white, like, T-shirt on, white T-shirt… I saw the white T-shirt and the guy just pulling back and running, running back."*
*(OCR lines 3951–3972)*

- Could **NOT** see the person's face
- Described race as: *"Not white, dark, but I don't know what race. Everything was orange from the fire."*
- The person was **approximately 50 yards away**
- Could only confirm: **white T-shirt**, **male**, **dark complexion**

**Cross-Examination by Mr. Goering — Critical Impeachment:**

> Q: "The white T-shirt that the person was wearing had short sleeves on it; is that right?"
> A: "Yes."
*(OCR line 4266–4269)*

> Q: "Isn't it true that you told that fire investigator that you were not able to tell if the person you saw was black, Mexican or white?"
> A: "I don't really remember saying or white. I just remember it was orange, it was orange, but dark."
*(OCR lines 4282–4329)*

> Q: "If the fire investigator that spoke with you made a note in his report that you told him you could not tell if the person you saw was black, Mexican or white — a white guy — would your testimony here today be he must have been mistaken in writing that down?"
> A: *"If he wrote that, it might have been, but I think I said I couldn't tell if it was Mexican or black or — but I know it wasn't white."*
*(OCR lines 4321–4329)*

> Q: "Did you tell someone who asked you what you had seen, that you believed the person that you saw running from the fire was a **14 to 20 year old Mexican youth**?"
> A: *"Might have been, but more black. I don't remember saying Mexican youth."*
*(OCR lines 4343–4347)*

**Night-of-fire statement to officer (card):**
> *"That one, I think I told him that I couldn't tell who, what, what race he was."*
*(OCR lines 4354–4355)*

**Other person Salazar saw that night:**
> *"I did also testify on direct examination, I believe, that you saw a heavy set, white male leaving the scene going toward…"*
*(OCR line 4392–4394 — Salazar's response cut off in OCR)*

## 2. VALERIE JOHNSON & PATRICIA DEVORCE — Description Given to Detective

Both women told the responding detective that Lenroy was wearing:

> *"White, tank top T-shirt and black jogging pants."*
> *"She indicated he had a Jamaican accent."*

*(OCR lines 10792–10794, 10758)*

Both Valerie and Patricia gave this description independently and it was consistent between them. They also reported that Lenroy had a **black Firebird** (OCR line 10702).

## 3. DETECTIVE — Eyewitness to Unknown Person at Scene

Before making contact with the complainants, the detective passed a person near the apartment complex on foot. He immediately ruled this person out as Lenroy:

> *"I passed a black male who was tall. In fact, he was my height, which is six foot two. He was skinny, like I am, thin build. He had on a **dark blue ball cap, baseball cap, and a light T-shirt type shirt. I believe it was light blue**, but I can only say for sure it was light."*

> *"I immediately identified that the **clothing description didn't match, the height didn't match**. When I passed him, I said hi, kind of to get a feel for whether or not this was a nervous individual or somebody who just happened to live there. He returned a hi back to me."*

> *"She [Valerie] indicated he had a Jamaican accent. I knew as far as physical description of the clothing was off. And when she said Jamaican accent, I had said hi to this person, **I did not hear the hi come back with a [Jamaican accent]**."*

*(OCR lines 10743–10798)*

**Ruling out this person as Lenroy:**
- Clothing: dark blue cap + light/light-blue T-shirt ≠ white tank top + black jogging pants
- Height did not match
- No Jamaican accent returned
- This person was **NOT further investigated or identified**

**Detective also identified Vernon Johnson at the scene** — establishing that Vernon was at the Park Lee Apartments that night and was NOT a tenant there.
*(OCR lines 11739–11744)*

## 4. VERNON JOHNSON INTERVIEW — Page 59 (Shirt Description)

During the pre-trial interview (the July 11, 1995 interview conducted by defense attorney Kurt Goering with Vernon Johnson), Vernon was questioned about a specific shirt:

> Q: *"Was it primarily white except for the writing and the bird that was on it?"*
> A: *"Yeah, it was red, blue, red, whi[te] and blue writing."*
> A: *"[Short sleeves.]"*

*(OCR lines 2849–2858)*

This describes a shirt that is:
- **Primarily white**
- Has a **bird graphic/design**
- Has **red and blue writing**
- **Short sleeves**

**This shirt is materially different from:**
- A plain white **tank top** (no design, no sleeves)
- A plain white T-shirt (no design)

The question was asked in the context of the night of the fire, suggesting this was the shirt Vernon — or possibly someone else — was wearing that night.

## 7. CRITICAL EVIDENCE GAPS & DEFENSE ARGUMENTS

### A. Salazar's description changed over time
Her initial statements (night of fire) said she could not determine race. A fire investigator's report may indicate she described the person as possibly white. By trial she testified "dark, not white" — but acknowledged she may have said she couldn't tell. A prior statement to an officer described a *"14 to 20 year old Mexican youth."*

### B. The tank top vs. T-shirt distinction
Valerie and Patricia described Lenroy in a **white tank top** (sleeveless). Salazar described a **white T-shirt with short sleeves**. These are two different garments. If Salazar was looking at the same person Valerie/Patricia described, the clothing doesn't match exactly.

### C. The unidentified 6'2" man
The detective saw a **6'2", thin black male in a dark blue cap and light blue T-shirt** near the scene and ruled him out as Lenroy because the clothing "didn't match." This person was never identified, never interviewed, and never explained. His presence near the fire scene at the time of the arson is unaccounted for.

### D. Vernon Johnson's shirt
Vernon was at the scene that night (the detective identified him there). Vernon's shirt (white, with bird graphic, red/blue writing, short sleeves) matches the general category of "white T-shirt" — but is a distinct, identifiable garment. **If Salazar saw Vernon, not Lenroy**, she would have seen a white short-sleeve shirt, consistent with her testimony.

### E. No physical clothing evidence recovered
No white tank top, white T-shirt, jogging pants, or any clothing belonging to Lenroy was recovered as physical evidence.
